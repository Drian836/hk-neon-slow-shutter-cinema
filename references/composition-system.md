# Composition System

## Composition Principle

Use composition to express proximity, obstruction, circulation, and emotional distance. The environment must carry narrative information rather than dissolve into a clean portrait background.

## Composition Grammar

## Spatial Layer Contract

Whenever plausible, identify:

1. **Foreground:** an occluder, reflective surface, passing body, vehicle edge, counter, door, curtain, or rail.
2. **Subject plane:** the emotional anchor and the information that must remain readable.
3. **Background:** circulation, practical source, architecture, or another person that explains the scene.

Do not add foreground clutter merely to satisfy the contract. A strong locked corridor or wall composition may use architecture as both foreground and route.

## Lens And Distance Feeling

- Use a close wide-angle feeling for distance-in-proximity: camera near the subject, exaggerated near/far relationship, deep environmental visibility.
- Use moderate wide or normal perspective for corridors, restaurants, and still tension.
- Use longer-lens compression sparingly for crowds or street layers when it preserves environmental density.
- Avoid default 85 mm beauty portrait language, creamy bokeh, perfect facial proportions through heavy correction, or a clean distant background.
- Do not specify an extreme lens number unless useful. Describe the visible spatial consequence first.
- In close wide-angle work, specify camera height, proximity, and which near object becomes larger than expected. Keep the face readable while tiles, rails, counters, shoulders, or vehicle edges exaggerate the near/far relationship.
- Wide angle is not merely a wider field of view. It must alter emotional distance: bodies feel physically close while routes, obstructions, or attention keep them apart.

## Camera Presence Contract

Choose one relationship before selecting palette:

- track with the subject while the world moves at a faster clock;
- lock near the subject while circulation crosses the lens;
- move with a vehicle while architecture and practical lights sweep past;
- observe through a threshold while subjects repeat or nearly cross a route;
- remain extremely close and off-axis while the room recedes disproportionately;
- share a cramped room and drift slightly with bodies rather than float as a neutral observer.

If a transformed photo preserves the exact source composition, background geometry, and camera relation without an explicit user request, composition has not participated in the transformation.

## Aspect Ratio

- Default to 3:2 or 16:9 landscape for a film-frame reading.
- Use 4:5 or vertical when the user requests portrait output, the source composition is vertical, or a corridor/stairwell benefits from it.
- Do not crop an edit target destructively merely to imitate a film ratio.
- Treat online screenshot ratios as potentially altered by transfer, restoration, player chrome, or crop.

## Relationship Staging

- Two people need not face one another. Use adjacent planes, opposed gaze, back-to-camera, doorway separation, or unequal depth.
- A missed encounter should show paths or sight lines that almost connect.
- Restrained intimacy should favor small gestures, shared practical light, and spatial barriers over overt romantic posing.
- Solitude should remain connected to city circulation; avoid an empty void unless the scene specifically calls for aftermath.

## Composition Selection Rules

- Choose one primary family and optionally one supporting device.
- Match the composition to the motion recipe. For example, do not pair a fully locked centered portrait with aggressive random handheld blur.
- Preserve the face or key object when identity matters, even if another plane is heavily occluded.
- In a batch, avoid repeating the same primary family on adjacent images.
- If the result feels staged, introduce a motivated foreground interruption, shift the subject off-axis, or reveal more working environment before adding more stylization.
