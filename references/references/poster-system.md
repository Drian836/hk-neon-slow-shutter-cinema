# Hong Kong Cinema Poster System

Use this only when the user asks for a poster, key art, title layout, cover, print design, or supplies poster references for design extraction. Do not apply poster typography to ordinary cinema-frame generation.

## Source Classification

Classify each reference before learning from it:

- **Film frame or photograph:** valid for camera, time, light, blocking, and film reproduction.
- **Official poster or key art:** valid for hierarchy, crop, title placement, credits, and print behavior; its exact assets remain protected residue.
- **Fan poster or editorial redesign:** valid only for proposed layout ideas; do not treat its invented typography or collage as original film evidence.
- **AI or provenance-uncertain image:** label uncertain and use only directly visible, source-safe design behavior.

Logos, distributor marks, festival laurels, exact credits, taglines, product labels, and copyrighted title artwork are never reusable unless the user supplies and owns the exact content or explicitly requests factual reproduction they are entitled to use.

## Poster Families

### Full-Bleed Character Memory

- one dominant portrait or environmental frame;
- face or body may be soft, partly cropped, reflected, or contaminated by practical light;
- title occupies clear negative space or crosses a low-information area;
- supporting text is sparse and subordinate.

### Mirrored Relationship

- two views, reflection, inverted portrait, or unequal paired faces express memory, doubling, or distance;
- the two images must differ in attention, scale, orientation, or temperature rather than become a symmetric duplication;
- title bridges or separates the pair.

### Temporal Editorial Montage

- two or three frames show different clocks, viewpoints, or relational states;
- panels may be stacked, cut by a hard band, or overlap with controlled crop;
- at least one panel carries lived-in environment, not only faces.

### Narrative Motif Collage

- one story-specific everyday motif repeats around a character or scene;
- use generic, source-safe objects unless the user provides owned assets;
- the motif supports plot or time, never exists only as decorative retro clutter.

### Blurred-Time Key Art

- a stable character zone sits against broken directional movement or a dragged practical source;
- typography stays sharp enough to anchor the unstable image;
- one dominant title gesture is enough.

## Typography

- Establish one primary title, one secondary bilingual title when useful, and one small information layer.
- Chinese may run vertically or horizontally according to the image's open route; English may use a restrained serif, condensed grotesk, or neutral grotesk.
- Do not imitate a specific released title lockup. Create new spacing, scale, line breaks, and alignment.
- Use no more than two type families and three meaningful size levels.
- Keep text clear of identity-critical eyes, hands, and emotional gestures unless purposeful occlusion is part of the design.
- Generate exact readable wording only when supplied by the user. Otherwise use no text or clearly labeled placeholder copy.

## Print Behavior

Use one coherent reproduction family:

- coarse offset or halftone dots with limited ink spread;
- soft color print with grain and modest misregistration;
- dark photographic print with restrained highlight bloom;
- muted archival paper with slightly uneven blacks.

Print texture must affect image and type consistently. A uniform noise overlay is insufficient.

## Poster Prompt Contract

State:

1. exact aspect ratio and intended use;
2. image hierarchy and poster family;
3. character/environment relationship;
4. time, camera, and lighting behavior in the image layer;
5. title wording, language, hierarchy, type behavior, and safe text zones;
6. print reproduction;
7. explicit exclusions: no copied title lockup, actor, logo, laurels, watermark, brand, or exact reference composition.

## Poster QA

- Poster reads at thumbnail size before small text is legible.
- Image layer would still express a relationship or time behavior without the color grade.
- Title and image form one hierarchy rather than competing layers.
- Bilingual text has deliberate alignment and spacing.
- No invented official logos, awards, credits, or product labels appear.
- No supplied reference is copied as a full layout.
- A poster reference is not misreported as proof of cinematography.
